# Suggested captions (numbers as computed by analysis/paper_figures.py)

**Figure 3. Gap sweep and warm-up transient (glass–glass, air).** (a) Through-gap voltage V_bot and the sum V_top + V_bot versus gap, mean of 3–5 runs per gap (error bars: run-to-run SD). V_bot falls 32% from 7.62 to 508 µm (67×), the sum only 1.9%. Open symbols: contact ABBA run for reference. (b) V_bot versus time for the 7.62 and 508 µm runs; shaded bands are the firmware plateau windows, read 7–10 min after heater-on while V_bot was still rising. The dashed curve is a 40-min ABBA phase at 508 µm.

**Figure 4. Gas conduction versus radiation across the 508 µm gap as a function of pressure (Simulation 1).** (a) Gas conductance (series continuum/free-molecular interpolation, α = 0.9) and the radiative conductance of each surface pair at 340 K. (b) Radiation share of gap heat. The February 2026 runs (shaded, 15–40 Torr given gauge accuracy) sit where gas conduction is still ~99% of its atmospheric value; radiation dominates only below about 10⁻² Torr.

**Figure 5. Atmospheric ABBA result and the rejected false positive (steel–glass, 508 µm, air).** (a) η_corrected on the sum metric (null, 1.0007 ± 0.0019) and the through-gap metric (0.9883 ± 0.0065, an apparent −1.17%); 95% CIs. (b) Heated-plate temperatures: the steel–glass stack runs 3.0 °C hotter than the glass–glass control (2.4–4.4 °C by phase). With a Bi₂Te₃ Seebeck temperature coefficient of 0.25–0.30 %/°C this predicts a 0.6–1.3% calibration artifact, matching the apparent signal, which was therefore rejected.

**Figure 6. Partial-vacuum ABBA (steel–glass, 508 µm, ~36 Torr).** (a) Through-gap phase curves. A1 (350.7 mV, 30–50 min) was not reproduced: the overnight A2 ran at deeper vacuum (306.3 mV) and the planned confirmation rerun on Feb 24 gave 385 ± 37 mV with pump cycling and is treated as inconclusive. (b) Glass–glass vacuum control: the forward phase was still rising (375 → 457 mV) inside its 20–40 min averaging window. (c) η_corrected for every analysis choice. Through-gap values are 0.83–0.99 and sum values 1.02–1.06: the direction of the asymmetry depends on the metric, so no rectification is claimed.

**Figure 7. What the gap alone can produce (Simulation 2).** Rectification between fixed surface temperatures for a steel emissivity that scales as T^n, at ~36 Torr (solid) and 10⁻⁵ mbar (dashed). With constant emissivities the gap gives exactly zero. At 36 Torr the gap contributes at most ~1% even at ΔT = 60 K, far below the measured asymmetries (shaded), which therefore cannot come from gap radiation.
